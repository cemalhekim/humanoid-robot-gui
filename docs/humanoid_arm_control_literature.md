# Literature survey: H1-2 arm tracking with arm_sdk (PD + feed-forward)

Local facts I checked first (they shape the recommendations): `server.py` sends `motor.dq = 0.0` on every arm_sdk command (lines 6407-6466), i.e. **no velocity feed-forward at all**; `ARM_SDK_KP = [120,120,80,50,50,50,50,…]`, `ARM_SDK_KD = [2,2,1.5,1,1,1,1,…]`; the outer PID runs at 60 Hz playback / 120 Hz hold; `ARM_REPLAY_INBAND_CORRECTION_SCALE = 0.0` leaves the gravity-learn integrator (gain 22, limit 4 Nm, `LEARN_LEAK_PER_S = 0.0`) alone inside the lock band (the comment at lines 278-281 already names this as the limit-cycle cause). The twin (`simulation/h1_2_twin/h1_2_mujoco_sim.py`) uses `unitree_mujoco/unitree_robots/h1_2/scene.xml` with **no `<option>` element → Euler integrator**, dt = 2 ms, `<motor>` actuators, default `damping=1 armature=0.1 frictionloss=0.2` on every joint, PD law applied every physics step, **no command/state latency modelled**.

## 1. PD + feed-forward tracking, gain rules, cascade stability

**Unitree `xr_teleoperate` H1_2 arm controller** (unitreerobotics, 2024-25, [robot_arm.py](https://github.com/unitreerobotics/xr_teleoperate/blob/main/teleop/robot_control/robot_arm.py), [robot_arm_ik.py](https://github.com/unitreerobotics/xr_teleoperate/blob/main/teleop/robot_control/robot_arm_ik.py)). This is the closest thing to a vendor reference law. For H1_2: `kp_low=140, kd_low=3` on shoulders/elbows, `kp_wrist=50, kd_wrist=2`, `control_dt = 1/250`, `tau = pin.rnea(model, data, q_sol, 0, 0)` (pure gravity feed-forward from the Pinocchio model, no Coriolis/inertia terms), a per-cycle velocity clamp `q_cmd = q_meas + delta/max(|delta|/(v_lim·dt),1)` with `v_lim = 30 rad/s` (so it is effectively a safety clamp, not a shaper), IK smoothness cost 0.1 on deviation from the previous solution plus a weighted moving-average filter on the solution. No outer PID, no integrator. Takeaway: Unitree runs the elbow at kp 140 vs. your 50, sends model-based gravity torque every cycle, and never integrates — their stack has no dead-band limit cycle by construction.

**Inertia-based PD gain selection** (standard second-order rule, restated for humanoid joints in recent Isaac Lab/MPC-RL papers, e.g. [arXiv 2606.05687](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.05687)): `Kp = J_eff·ωn²`, `Kd = 2ζ·J_eff·ωn`, so `Kd_crit = 2√(Kp·J_eff)`, with `J_eff` = diagonal rigid-body inertia seen at the joint plus reflected rotor inertia (armature). Back-of-envelope for H1-2: shoulder pitch with the arm outstretched has J_eff ≈ 1-1.5 kg m² → Kd_crit ≈ 2√(120·1.2) ≈ 24; your kd = 2 gives ζ ≈ 0.08. Elbow J_eff ≈ 0.15-0.2 → Kd_crit ≈ 6; kd = 1 gives ζ ≈ 0.17. These loops are strongly underdamped in the rigid-body sense and rely on gearbox friction for damping, which explains oscillation precisely when gravity assists the motion (descending) and the friction/gravity sign relationship changes. MuJoCo can compute this number for you: the `position` actuator's `dampratio` attribute is defined in units of `2√(kp·m)` with `m` evaluated at `qpos0` including armature ([MuJoCo XML reference](https://mujoco.readthedocs.io/en/stable/XMLreference.html#actuator-position)).

**Computed-torque / RNEA feed-forward** (Pinocchio docs, [Inverse dynamics](https://docs.ros.org/en/jazzy/p/pinocchio/doc/a-features/g-dynamic.html); Carpentier et al., "The Pinocchio C++ library", SII 2019). `pin.rnea(q, v, a)` gives `M(q)a + C(q,v)v + g(q)`; `rnea(q,0,0)` is gravity only, `rnea(q_d, v_d, a_d)` is full computed-torque feed-forward, ~1 µs per call for a 7-DoF arm. Exoskeleton and cobot studies consistently find the inertial term matters only during fast accelerations; for 0.6 rad/s peak motions gravity + Coriolis dominate and the cheap win is the gravity term evaluated on the *desired* trajectory rather than filtered measured torque (which contains the PD reaction and lags by your 0.4 s filter).

**Cascade rules of thumb** ([MathWorks cascade design](https://www.mathworks.com/help/control/ug/designing-cascade-control-system-with-pi-controllers.html); [arXiv 2203.09022](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2203.09022) on minimum bandwidth ratio; [arXiv 2601.18639](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.18639) on discrete-time PID with saturation and delay). Inner loop should be 5-10× faster than the outer loop; sampling period and computational delay directly eat phase margin, and 5-25 ms is the typical delay band in articulated robots. The inner motor PD at 500 Hz with kp 120 has a bandwidth of a few Hz (√(kp/J)/2π ≈ 1.6 Hz at the shoulder), so a 60 Hz outer loop is not rate-limited, but the *inner loop is the slow one* — the outer PID cannot add bandwidth, only fight the inner loop's lag. This is the structural cause of the 0.5-0.8 s lag: you have two slow loops in series and no feed-forward of velocity.

## 2. Anti-windup, dead-band limit cycles, friction

**Caparroza, Soltesz, Hägglund, Guzmán, "Anti-Windup in PID Control: Review, Analysis, and New Tuning Directions", 2026** ([arXiv 2606.01959](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.01959v1)). Compares dynamic and instantaneous back-calculation, conditional integration, and hybrids. Key result: the optimal tracking time constant is generally *smaller* than the classical `Tt = Ti` or `√(Ti·Td)` rules, and depends on saturation ratio; no single scheme is universally best. Practical takeaway for the gravity-learn term: back-calculation with `Tt` ≈ 0.1-0.3 s, or conditional integration gated on |error| ∧ |velocity| (which you partly have via `LEARN_ARM_STATIONARY_GATE`, currently 0).

**Beerens, Bisoffi, Zaccarian, Heemels, Nijmeijer, van de Wouw, "Reset integral control for improved settling of PID-based motion systems with friction", Automatica 107, 2019** ([PDF](https://heemels.tue.nl/content/papers/BeeBis_AUT19a.pdf)). Proves that PID + Coulomb/static friction converges to a set of equilibria `|z3| ≤ Fs/ki` (the integrator can sit anywhere inside the stiction band), and that the linear integrator's "depletion and refilling" upon overshoot causes long stick phases. The reset law `z3+ = −α·z3 − (1+α)·(kp/ki)·e` fires on overshoot detection (velocity sign opposite to the PI force). Equivalent reading for your case: an integrator acting alone inside a dead band with a friction-laden plant *will* hunt; the fix is either to stop integrating when |e| is below the friction-equivalent error, or reset/leak the integrator on zero-velocity crossings.

**Ruderman, "Stick-slip and convergence of feedback-controlled systems with Coulomb friction", Asian J. Control 2022** ([arXiv 2006.08977](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.08977)) and Armstrong-Hélouvry, Dupont, Canudas de Wit, "A survey of models, analysis tools and compensation methods for the control of machines with friction", Automatica 1994; Hensen et al. 2003 ([Friction-induced hunting limit cycles](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0005109803002346)). Describing-function analysis of dead-zone + integrator predicts hunting only qualitatively; the algebraic stiction-region result is more useful: the region width scales with `Fs/kp`, so raising kp (or gravity feed-forward accuracy) shrinks it, raising ki only speeds up the cycle. Dither (small high-frequency torque oscillation) and pulse-based compensation are the classical non-model alternatives; on a 500 Hz DDS link dither above ~50 Hz is not realisable, so Stribeck feed-forward is the practical path.

**Integral dead-band** (McMillan, ISA PID tuning appendices, [PDF](https://www.isa.org/getmedia/e5c068e9-57ea-4ebf-b806-c25ee4e3e081/PID-Tuning-Appendices_WEB_PDF.pdf)): set the integral dead-band equal to the observed limit-cycle amplitude of the measured variable — a one-line, one-parameter fix that is directly testable in the twin.

## 3. Trajectory shaping for low lag

**Velocity feed-forward** (PMD, "Feedforward in motion control" ([link](https://www.pmdcorp.com/resources/type/articles/get/feedforward-in-motion-control)); servo-loop practice). Feeding `dq_d` into the inner loop's velocity term removes velocity-proportional following error `e ≈ v/Kv_eff`; acceleration feed-forward removes the acceleration-proportional part. Unitree's `motor_cmd.dq` *is* this input: the motor law is `τ = τ_ff + kp(q_d − q) + kd(dq_d − dq)` (confirmed in `unitree_sdk2py_bridge.py` and the Unitree comm.h field semantics, [MYBOTSHOP forum](https://forum.mybotshop.de/t/unitree-go1-low-level-command-values/460)). Sending `dq = 0` turns kd into pure drag on your own trajectory: at 0.6 rad/s and kd = 2 that is 1.2 Nm of opposing torque at the shoulder, which the outer PID then has to integrate away — a direct source of lag and of wind-up.

**Minimum-jerk / S-curve generation** (Ghazaei Ardakani, Robertsson, Johansson, "Online minimum-jerk trajectory generation", IMA 2015, [PDF](https://cdn.ima.org.uk/wp/wp-content/uploads/2017/12/Ghazaei-Ardakani-Robertsson-Johansson-Online-Minimum-Jerk-Trajectory-Generation.pdf); jerk-constrained TOTP, [arXiv 2404.07889](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.07889v1)). Since your approach profile is already smooth (`ARM_REPLAY_APPROACH_PEAK_VEL_RAD_S = 0.6`, ≥2 s), the analytic derivative of the same profile is free and exactly the `dq_d` to send; a phase-lead (sampling the reference `Δt` ahead, `Δt` ≈ measured inner-loop lag) is the standard preview compensation.

**Input shaping** (Thomsen, Søe-Knudsen et al., "Vibration control of industrial robot arms by multi-mode time-varying input shaping", MMT 2020; review in [Extrica](https://www.extrica.com/article/16725)). ZV/ZVD shapers cancel a known closed-loop mode at the cost of half/one period of delay. Relevant only if the descending oscillation has a stable frequency; at ζ ≈ 0.1 and f ≈ 1.5 Hz a ZV shaper costs 0.33 s of extra lag — likely worse than fixing the damping. Keep as fallback.

**Setpoint re-basing** (xr_teleoperate velocity clamp above; Unitree DDS `arm_sdk` weight ramp). The clamp `q_cmd = q_meas + sat(Δ)` is a first-order lag whose time constant is state-dependent; it suppresses jumps but adds lag proportional to the clamp. Your 2 rad/s `TRAJECTORY_MAX_VELOCITY_RAD_S` is above the 0.6 rad/s profile peak, so it should not be the lag source; the 0.4 s gravity filter and `dq = 0` are.

## 4. Sim-to-real at the joint level

**Hwangbo et al., "Learning agile and dynamic motor skills for legged robots", Science Robotics 4(26), 2019** ([arXiv 1901.08652](https://arxiv.org/abs/1901.08652)). Actuator net: MLP 3×32 softsign, input = position error and velocity at `t, t−0.01, t−0.02` s, output torque; trained on 4 min of sine-excited data at 400 Hz (amplitude 5-10 cm, 1-25 Hz — "excitation must cover a wide range of frequency spectra"); validation RMS 0.7-0.8 Nm; the "ideal actuator" (no delay, infinite bandwidth) fails. The history window must exceed "the sum of all communication delays and the mechanical response time". For your problem: even without training a net, this tells you what the twin is missing — the delay and the finite bandwidth — and gives the recipe to log `(q_d − q, dq, tau_est)` on the real arm with the same excitation.

**Domain-randomisation ranges actually used on H1/G1 in 2024-25**: OmniH2O (He et al., CoRL 2024, [arXiv 2406.08858](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.08858)): P/D gains U(0.75,1.25)×default, control delay U(20,60) ms, friction U(0.2,1.1), link mass U(0.7,1.3), torque RFI 0.1×limit. HumanPlus (Fu et al., CoRL 2024, [arXiv 2406.10454](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.10454v1)): control delay [0.02,0.04] s, motor strength [0.8,1.1]. TWIST (Ze et al., 2025, [arXiv 2505.02833](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.02833)): friction [0.1,2.0], motor strength [0.8,1.2], and a reported 0.9 s end-to-end teleop latency. The human2humanoid H1 config ([h1_teleop_config.py](https://github.com/LeCAR-Lab/human2humanoid/blob/main/legged_gym/legged_gym/envs/h1/h1_teleop_config.py)) uses shoulder/elbow kp 100, kd 2, `ctrl_delay_step_range=[1,3]`, sim dt 5 ms, decimation 4. The consensus is that **20-60 ms of command delay is the realistic band for Unitree DDS + motor loop**, and every stack that transferred to hardware randomised it.

**Friction identification from torque estimates**: Gilbert, Maina, Byiringiro, "Nonlinear Parameter Identification of the Franka Emika PANDA Robot: A Comparative Analysis of Friction Models", AJEEET 4(2), 2024 ([PDF](https://pdfs.semanticscholar.org/793a/57d781ed4cacdb2d6b641d4595eb7219c9ab.pdf)) — constrained least squares with Lasso on `tau_J`; Stribeck beats LuGre and sigmoidal. Sorrentino et al., "Physics-Informed Learning for the Friction Modeling of High-Ratio Harmonic Drives", Humanoids 2024 ([arXiv 2410.12685](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.12685)) — identifies Coulomb-viscous / Stribeck / PINN friction from motor current, q, dq on ergoCub, then feeds it forward in a two-layer torque controller. Both are directly applicable to H1-2 `tau_est` logs (which are already current-based).

**Actuator Reality Shaping** (Yamamori et al., 2026, [arXiv 2607.02205](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.02205)): instead of matching sim to hardware, a per-joint 2-DoF feed-forward/feedback controller makes the *real* actuator behave like the sim's ideal second-order response. Validated on a 7-DoF arm and a humanoid. This is philosophically what your outer loop is doing; the paper's point is that the reference-shaping half should be pure feed-forward (velocity/acceleration) and the feedback half should be tuned for robustness only.

## 5. Recent humanoid manipulation/teleop stacks: what sits between IK and motors

Open-TeleVision (Cheng et al., CoRL 2024, [arXiv 2407.01512](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.01512)): Pinocchio CLIK at 60 Hz, SE(3) filter on wrist targets, joint targets to H1 arms, no outer PID, gains unpublished. HumanPlus: 50 Hz policy → 1000 Hz PD, gains unpublished. OmniH2O / human2humanoid: 50 Hz policy → PD, shoulder/elbow kp 100 kd 2 (sim). TWIST: 50 Hz → 1 kHz PD. Mobile ALOHA (Fu et al. 2024) uses Interbotix/Dynamixel position servos — no comparable law. Figure/Tesla/1X publish nothing actionable. Unitree's own `xr_teleoperate` (Section 1) is the only source with concrete H1-2 arm_sdk numbers: 250 Hz, kp 140/50, kd 3/2, gravity RNEA feed-forward, no integrator. The G1 arm overheating issue ([unitree_sdk2_python #129](https://github.com/unitreerobotics/unitree_sdk2_python/issues/129)) shows kp 40 / kd 1.5 without gravity feed-forward overheats the shoulder — an argument for model feed-forward rather than integrator-built holding torque. Pattern across all of them: **no outer position PID; a smooth reference at 50-250 Hz plus joint PD plus (at most) gravity feed-forward.**

## 6. MuJoCo specifics

[MuJoCo Computation chapter](https://mujoco.readthedocs.io/en/stable/computation/index.html): "The recommended integrator is `implicitfast`", treating damping and actuator velocity-dependent forces implicitly; Euler is "for compatibility with older models". For `position` actuators with `kv`, the XML reference explicitly says "it is recommended to use the implicitfast or implicit integrators". Note the catch for your bridge: your PD is computed in Python and injected as `ctrl` on a `<motor>`, so MuJoCo cannot treat the kd term implicitly — at kd = 2 and dt = 2 ms this is stable, but any kd sweep toward critical damping (kd ≈ 20) will be numerically pessimistic under Euler. The `position` actuator (`biasprm = 0, −kp, −kv`, optional `dyntype=filterexact` with `timeconst`) gives you an implicit, first-order-filtered PD for free, and `general` actuators now support `dyntype=pid` with `ki`, `imax` (anti-windup clamp) and `slewmax` (setpoint rate limit) natively. `actearly=true` removes one time step of activation delay. `armature` is the reflected rotor inertia (`I_rotor·N²`); H1-2 shoulder actuators are ~40 Nm class with roughly 10:1 planetary — the MJCF default `armature=0.1` is a guess, and `frictionloss=0.2` Nm is far below what a 40 Nm gearbox actually has (Stribeck fits on comparable QDD joints give 0.5-2 Nm Coulomb). The `<default>` block at the top of `h1_2_handless.xml` is where to override these.

# Addendum — second pass (same survey, refined; adds critical-damping gain rule, vendor-gain baseline, ASAP, timestep-invariance check)


Context recap for the reader: the arm motors run `tau = tau_ff + kp(q_des − q) + kd(dq_des − dq)` on the robot (Unitree's motor-command semantics; identically implemented in the twin's `unitree_sdk2py_bridge.py` and in `simulation/h1_2_twin/h1_2_mujoco_sim.py`). `server.py` today sends `q`, `tau_ff`, `kp`, `kd` and **always `dq = 0`** (lines ~6407–6466), so the inner kd acts as pure damping and every trajectory is tracked by the outer 60/120 Hz PID alone. This single fact explains a large part of problems (1) and (3), see topic 1/3.

Important twin facts found while surveying: `unitree_robots/h1_2/h1_2_handless.xml` uses `<joint damping="1" armature="0.1" frictionloss="0.2">` for *all* joints, `<motor>` actuators with `ctrlrange` = ±40/18/19 Nm, no `<option>` element (so the default **Euler** integrator, 2 ms timestep), no command latency model, and the bridge PD law is evaluated once per DDS message, not per physics step (the local twin evaluates per physics step). Note the MJCF caps shoulder torque at 40 Nm whereas Unitree quotes 120 Nm peak for H1-2 arm joints, so saturation behaviour in the twin is pessimistic.

## 1. PD + feed-forward tracking on cobots/humanoids

**Unitree `xr_teleoperate` `robot_arm.py` (Unitree Robotics, 2024-2026, github.com/unitreerobotics/xr_teleoperate)** — the vendor reference for H1-2 arms. `H1_2_ArmController`: `kp_high=300/kd_high=5`, `kp_low=140/kd_low=3` for shoulder+elbow ("weak motors"), `kp_wrist=50/kd_wrist=2`; 250 Hz loop; `tau` feed-forward sent; **velocity clamp on the target**: `motion_scale = max|Δ| /(velocity_limit·dt)`; `clipped_target = current_q + Δ/max(motion_scale,1)`, i.e. the setpoint is re-based on the *measured* q every cycle with a 30 rad/s limit. No integrator, no outer PID. Companion `robot_arm_ik.py` computes `tau_ff = pin.rnea(model, data, q_sol, 0, 0)` (pure gravity term) and applies a weighted moving-average filter to the IK solution. Takeaway: Unitree's own stack is *inner PD with kp 140–300 on shoulder/elbow + model gravity feed-forward + setpoint re-basing*, roughly 2–3x stiffer than our `ARM_SDK_KP=[120,120,80,50,…]` and with no outer integrator at all.

**MuJoCo XML reference, `position/dampratio` (DeepMind, mujoco.readthedocs.io)** — codifies the inertia-based gain rule: `kd = 2ζ·sqrt(kp·m_eff)`, with `m_eff` taken from the diagonal of the mass matrix at `qpos0` including armature. ζ=1 is critical damping. Same rule appears as "effective-inertia PD gain selection" `Kp = J_eff·ωn², Kd = 2ζ·J_eff·ωn` in recent humanoid RL deployment papers (e.g. arXiv 2606.05687, 2026). Takeaway: compute `M(q)` diagonals with Pinocchio/`mj_fullM` for the H1-2 arm; with `kp=120` and shoulder-pitch `m_eff`≈0.5–1.0 kg·m² (arm outstretched) the critical kd is ≈15–22, yet ours is 2.0 (ζ≈0.1–0.15). That is a textbook recipe for the descending-motion oscillation in problem (1): gravity drives the joint, the PD is heavily underdamped, and the outer PID adds phase lag.

**Pinocchio RNEA docs / "The Pinocchio C++ library" (Carpentier et al., 2019, SII)** — `pin.rnea(model,data,q,v,a)` returns the full inverse-dynamics torque in ~1 µs per call; with `v=a=0` it is exactly the gravity vector, with the trajectory's `v, a` it is computed-torque feed-forward. Takeaway: the feed-forward for problem (3) is one call away, and we already have the URDF model in `kinematics.gravity_torques`.

**Cascade-loop rule of thumb (control.com / MathWorks cascade design pages; Constraint-Aware Discrete-Time PID Gain Optimization…, arXiv 2601.18639, 2026)** — inner loop must be 5–10x faster (bandwidth) than the outer loop or the outer loop "sees" inner dynamics and loses phase margin; sampling period and 5–25 ms delays "reduce phase margin" and can turn a benign continuous tuning unstable. Takeaway: our inner PD with ζ≈0.1 has a resonant, *not* fast, response; the 60 Hz outer PID with 0.4 s gravity-filter lag is then well inside the inner loop's dynamics. Fixing ζ first is a prerequisite for any outer-loop tuning.

## 2. Anti-windup, dead-band limit cycles, friction

**Anti-Windup in PID Control: Review, Analysis, and New Tuning Directions (Caparroza, Soltesz, Hägglund, Guzmán, 2026, arXiv 2606.01959)** — compares dynamic/instantaneous back-calculation, conditional integration and hybrids; finds the optimal back-calculation tracking time `Tt` is "generally smaller than the integral time", not the classical `Tt = sqrt(Ti·Td)`. Takeaway: our `ARM_REPLAY_INTEGRAL_LIMIT`/`GRAVITY_LEARN_LIMIT` clamping is the weakest scheme; back-calculation against the *saturating quantity* (the ±0.12 rad `MAX_PID_CORRECTION` clamp and the gravity tau limits) unwinds the integrator during motion instead of letting it pin at the limit (problem 4).

**Reset integral control for improved settling of PID-based motion systems with friction (Beerens, Bisoffi, Zaccarian, Heemels, Nijmeijer, van de Wouw, Automatica 107, 2019)** — with Coulomb/static friction and integral action, the equilibrium set is a *band* `|z3| ≤ Fs/ki` and the linear integrator "depletes and refills" on every overshoot, producing hunting; a Clegg-style reset `z3+ = −α z3 − (1+α)(kp/ki)(z1 − r)` on detected overshoot-and-stick eliminates it. Also: Ruderman, *Stick-slip and convergence of feedback-controlled systems with Coulomb friction*, Asian J. Control 2022 (arXiv 2006.08977) proves a PID with Coulomb friction converges only through "an infinite number of consecutive stick-slip cycles". Takeaway for problem (2): an integrator that keeps integrating while the joint sits inside a dead band with zero P/D correction (`ARM_REPLAY_INBAND_CORRECTION_SCALE=0`) *must* limit-cycle; the fixes are integral dead-band (McMillan/ISA: set it equal to the limit-cycle amplitude), integrator freeze inside the band, or reset on error sign change.

**Friction identification: Gilbert, Maina, Byiringiro, *Nonlinear Parameter Identification of the Franka Emika PANDA Robot: A Comparative Analysis of Friction Models*, Andalas J. EEET 2024; Sorrentino et al., *Physics-Informed Learning for the Friction Modeling of High-Ratio Harmonic Drives*, Humanoids 2024 (arXiv 2410.12685)** — Stribeck-Coulomb-viscous fitted by constrained least squares on measured joint torque outperforms LuGre/sigmoid; the ergoCub work identifies friction purely from motor current (`tau_est`), q, dq and feeds it into a two-layer torque controller. Takeaway: the H1-2 `tau_est` stream plus slow constant-velocity sweeps gives a per-joint `F_c, F_v, F_s, v_s` table in one evening; the twin's uniform `frictionloss=0.2` can then be replaced per joint.

**Armstrong-Hélouvry, Dupont, Canudas de Wit, *A survey of models, analysis tools and compensation methods for the control of machines with friction*, Automatica 1994** — canonical reference for dither and describing-function analysis; the follow-up literature (Automatica 1995 "PID control in the presence of static friction: algebraic vs describing function") shows DF predictions of stick-slip are unreliable, so simulate rather than compute.

## 3. Trajectory shaping / low-lag tracking

**Feedforward in motion control (PMD Corp; control.com; Isaac Sim gain-tuning tutorial)** — velocity (and acceleration) feed-forward removes the velocity-proportional following error that a pure P position loop needs to generate its drive; the classic drawback ("Kp must then be lowered") disappears when the inner loop already has a kd term that accepts `dq_des`. Takeaway: our 0.5–0.8 s lag is the expected steady-state lag of a P loop at `kp_outer≈0.28` following a ramp; sending the trajectory's `dq` in the `dq` field (velocity feed-forward through the motor kd) plus RNEA `tau_ff` should remove most of it with no outer gain change.

**Input shaping reviews (Thomsen et al., *Vibration control of industrial robot arms by multi-mode time-varying input shaping*, Mech. Mach. Theory 2021; Extrica review 2016)** — ZV/ZVD shapers cancel a known mode at the cost of half/one vibration period of delay; ZVD is robust to ±20 % frequency error. Takeaway: only worth it if experiment E1 shows a residual mode; the underdamped inner PD *is* the mode (ω≈sqrt(kp/m_eff)≈11–15 rad/s → period ~0.5 s, so a ZV shaper adds 0.25 s lag — worse than fixing kd).

**Setpoint re-basing (xr_teleoperate, above; Open-TeleVision, Cheng et al., CoRL 2024, arXiv 2407.01512)** — TeleVision runs the whole loop at 60 Hz, SE(3)-filters wrist poses and sends absolute joint targets from Pinocchio CLIK; xr_teleoperate re-bases the target on measured q every cycle with a per-cycle velocity clamp. Takeaway: re-basing is a first-order lag *without an integrator*, so there is no wind-up during motion; it is the vendor's answer to problem (4).

## 4. Sim-to-real for joint-level control

**Hwangbo et al., *Learning agile and dynamic motor skills for legged robots*, Science Robotics 4(26), 2019** — actuator net: MLP 3×32 softsign, inputs = position error and velocity at t, t−0.01, t−0.02 s, trained on 4 min of 400 Hz data from sine foot trajectories (5–10 cm, 1–25 Hz), validation RMS 0.7–0.8 Nm; the "ideal actuator" (no delay, infinite bandwidth) "fails to produce a reasonable prediction"; history must be "longer than the sum of all communication delays and the mechanical response time". Takeaway: we can log (`q_des, q, dq, tau_est`) from the real arm under chirps and fit the same 3-tap MLP per joint; in the twin it replaces `kp(q_des−q)+kd(dq_des−dq)` in the bridge. Minimal version: fit only a delay + first-order torque filter.

**OmniH2O (He et al., CoRL 2024, arXiv 2406.08858) + human2humanoid `h1_teleop_config.py`; HumanPlus (Fu et al., CoRL 2024, arXiv 2406.10454)** — published H1 domain-randomisation ranges: PD gains ×U(0.75,1.25), control delay U(20,60) ms (config: 1–3 policy steps at 50 Hz), friction U(0.2,1.1)/[-0.6,1.2] range, link mass ×U(0.7,1.3), torque RFI 0.1×limit; HumanPlus: motor strength [0.8,1.1], control delay [0.02,0.04] s, EE payload [0,0.5] kg. Their H1 arm gains: shoulder/elbow `kp=100, kd=2` (policy 50 Hz, PD 1000 Hz). Takeaway: an arm PD tuned by CMA-ES in the twin should be scored under exactly these perturbations (delay 20–60 ms is the big one for us); the vendor and RL communities converge on kp≈100–300 for the H1 shoulder.

**Actuator Reality Shaping for Zero-Shot Sim-to-Real Robot Learning (Yamamori et al., 2026, arXiv 2607.02205)** — inverse of the actuator-net idea: put a 2-DoF feed-forward/feedback controller on each real joint so its closed loop matches the idealised second-order reference used in sim. Takeaway: same recipe as our situation — choose (kp, kd) for a target ωn, ζ and add feed-forward, then the twin and robot agree by construction.

## 6. MuJoCo specifics

**MuJoCo docs, Computation → Numerical integration; XML reference `option/integrator`, `position/kv,dampratio`, `general/actearly`, `joint/armature,frictionloss`** — "The recommended integrator is `implicitfast` which usually has the best tradeoff of stability and performance"; `kv`: "When using this attribute, it is recommended to use the implicitfast or implicit integrators"; `Euler` only "for compatibility with older models". `implicitfast` integrates joint damping *and actuator velocity feedback* implicitly, so a stiff PD can run at 2 ms without numerical ringing; `<position ... kp kv/dampratio timeconst>` adds a first-order setpoint filter; `actearly` removes one step of actuator delay; `frictionloss` is a proper dry-friction constraint (solver-handled, so no stick-slip chatter); `armature` = reflected rotor inertia. Takeaway: our twin runs Euler with the PD computed as an explicit external torque, so it cannot tell numerical ringing from physical underdamping. Either switch the arms to `<position kp kv>` + `implicitfast` (kv then implicit) or keep `<motor>` + external PD but set `integrator="implicitfast"` and confirm the response is timestep-invariant (1 ms vs 2 ms). A `dyntype="filter"`/`filterexact` general actuator with `timeconst`≈5–10 ms is the cheapest stand-in for motor-driver torque bandwidth; a ring buffer of 10–30 ms in the bridge is the cheapest stand-in for DDS latency.

## Ranked experiments for tonight (MuJoCo twin, no robot)

Metrics: `sweep_arm_workspace` cost (mean + CVaR10) already used by `optimize_params.py`, plus three new logged scalars: settling time to ±0.01 rad, overshoot on descending moves, and phase lag (cross-correlation shift between q_des and q) on a 0.3 Hz sinusoid.

1. **Critical-damping inner gains.** Hypothesis: problem (1) is ζ≈0.1 on the inner PD. Change: compute `m_eff` per arm joint from `mj_fullM` at 3 poses, set `ARM_SDK_KD = 2·ζ·sqrt(ARM_SDK_KP·m_eff)` with ζ∈{0.7,1.0}, keep kp. Metric: descending-move overshoot and settle time. Effort: 1 h. (Parameter-only.)
2. **Vendor gains as baseline.** Hypothesis: kp 140/300 kd 3/5 (xr_teleoperate H1-2) with outer PID *off* already beats our stack on the motion bank. Change: `ARM_SDK_KP/KD` override, `ARM_REPLAY_PID_GAINS`→0, gravity model scale 1.0. Metric: bank cost, lag. Effort: 30 min. (Parameter-only.)
3. **`implicitfast` + timestep invariance check.** Hypothesis: part of the twin's oscillation is Euler artefact. Change: `model.opt.integrator = mjINT_IMPLICITFAST` in `h1_2_mujoco_sim.py`; run bank at dt 1 ms and 2 ms. Metric: cost difference between dt's must drop below noise. Effort: 20 min. (Sim-only.)
4. **Velocity feed-forward through `dq`.** Hypothesis: 0.5–0.8 s lag is ramp-following error of the P loop. Change: send trajectory `dq_des` in the `dq` field (currently 0). Metric: lag on sinusoid, bank cost. Effort: 1 h. (Needs server.py change.)
5. **RNEA computed-torque feed-forward.** Hypothesis: with `tau_ff = rnea(q_d, dq_d, ddq_d)` from the URDF (Pinocchio or `mj_inverse`), outer PID gains can drop 2–3x without lag. Change: replace measured-torque gravity estimate with model term (`GRAVITY_MODEL_SCALE=1`) and add `M·ddq + C` terms. Metric: lag, RMS error, integrator magnitude. Effort: 2 h. (Needs server.py change; the scale=1 part is parameter-only.)
6. **Integrator dead-band / freeze / reset.** Hypothesis: problem (2) is the gravity-learn integrator running inside the lock band with zero P/D. Change: (a) freeze learning when `|e|<band`, (b) Beerens reset on sign change of e·ė, (c) `ARM_REPLAY_INBAND_CORRECTION_SCALE`=0.3 so the band is not open-loop. Metric: limit-cycle amplitude over a 20 s hold. Effort: 1 h for (c), 2 h for (a)/(b). ((c) parameter-only; (a),(b) need server.py.)
7. **Back-calculation anti-windup on gravity learn.** Hypothesis: problem (4) is clamp-type windup. Change: `learn += gain·e − (1/Tt)·(u_unsat − u_sat)`, `Tt`≈0.3·Ti; or gate learning on |dq|<0.05 rad/s (`LEARN_ARM_STATIONARY_GATE=1`, `LEARN_LEAK_PER_S`>0 already exist). Metric: learned-torque overshoot after a fast move. Effort: 30 min for gating (parameter-only), 1.5 h for back-calc (server.py).
8. **Latency + delay robustness in the twin.** Hypothesis: gains tuned at zero latency will oscillate on the real 500 Hz DDS path. Change: add a 10/20/40 ms command ring buffer and a `filter` torque actuator (`timeconst` 5 ms) in the twin; rerun 1, 2 under each. Metric: which gain set keeps ζ_eff>0.5 at 40 ms. Effort: 1.5 h. (Sim-only.)
9. **Per-joint friction sweep (twin-side).** Hypothesis: `frictionloss=0.2` everywhere hides the real stick band. Change: sweep frictionloss 0–1.0 Nm on shoulder/elbow and armature 0.05–0.3; record the hold limit-cycle amplitude vs I-gain. Metric: map of (friction, ki) → limit cycle. Effort: 1 h. (Sim-only; feeds a real `tau_est` identification later.)
10. **CMA-ES re-run under OmniH2O-style randomisation.** Change: for each trial randomise PD ×U(0.75,1.25), delay U(20,60) ms, mass ×U(0.7,1.3); fitness = mean+CVaR10 as now. Metric: robust optimum vs current optimum. Effort: 2 h after 8. (Sim-only.)
