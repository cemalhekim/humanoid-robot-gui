Learn the console

Ninety seconds of tutorials — everything the dashboard can do, with the exact clicks.

Move the arms

Pose the digital twin, press one button, the robot follows with a PID-controlled motion.

step by step
  1. Open High Level Controller.
  2. Drag the blue/red wrist spheres — the red twin is your target pose.
  3. Click Move. No saving needed; Save Pose is optional for reuse.
  4. Watch the live twin close the gap — the closed-loop PID holds the pose dead still.

Twist the torso

The green waist ring rotates the torso — combined arm + torso poses in one Move.

step by step
  1. In High Level Controller, grab the green ring around the waist.
  2. Turn it — the knob marks the front.
  3. Click Move; arms and torso travel together.

See what the robot sees

A floating camera bubble lives on every page — head camera plus the USB webcam, stacked.

step by step
  1. Click the round red camera button (bottom-right, any page).
  2. Drag the header to move it; pull a corner to resize.
  3. ▾ shrinks it back to the bubble. Both feeds reconnect on their own.

Drive around

Guarded locomotion presets — walk, strafe and turn from the floating drive bubble.

step by step
  1. Open Loco Control — the Drive bubble appears.
  2. Arrows walk/strafe; curved arrows turn.
  3. Stop Move halts locomotion instantly.

Ask the assistant

A read-only AI that answers from live telemetry — temperatures, health, orientation.

step by step
  1. On the Dashboard, type into the Command Center — or hold the mic to talk.
  2. Try “which motor is hottest?” or “is the robot upright?”.
  3. It can read everything, and move nothing — by design.

Teleoperate in XR

Vision Pro teleoperation with selectable control styles — pad, head rotation or position matching.

step by step
  1. Open Teleoperation and pick a control method card.
  2. The VR View opens for the headset (same Wi-Fi).
  3. Torso Sync is coming soon — the locked fourth card.

Safety, three buttons

Top-right of the dashboard, always in reach. Know these before anything else.

Release

Everything goes limp. Damps every motor instantly — the arms will sag, so make sure the robot is supported. This is your “let go now”.

Stand Up

Recover after Release. Re-engages the locked stand (the RC left+up combo, in software) so the robot holds itself again.

Home

Freeze the arms. Holds them exactly where they are with the closed-loop PID — no motion, just stiffness. A calm pause button.

If something looks wrong

Camera is dark right after a reboot

It heals itself: the camera service now waits for the robot’s video source and re-applies its config on every start. Give it one to two minutes after power-on. If it is still dark afterwards, the floating bubble’s dot stays gray — then ask the operator to check the TeleImager service.

The UI stutters or video drops on Wi-Fi

The robot’s Wi-Fi adapter is the weakest link in the chain. Switch to the Ethernet entrance — wired is sub-millisecond and immune to the drops.

Robot answers ping but the dashboard won’t load

That is almost always the Wi-Fi link, not the robot. Connect the operator laptop by cable to the 192.168.123.x network and use the Ethernet entrance; the dashboard on the robot itself will be running fine.

A Move doesn’t reach the exact pose

Small residuals of a degree or two on outstretched arms are gravity droop — physics, not a bug. If a joint stops far from its target, the pose probably collides with the robot’s own body; the controller detects the contact and backs off instead of pressing.

Where did the hands go?

The RH56BFX Hands card on the dashboard has an On/Off toggle. Off grays the card and hides the hands on every digital twin. Flip it back on any time — the choice is remembered per browser.

Robot switched off? The Wi-Fi and Ethernet entrances will be dark — use Offline (read-only) to keep working, or bookmark this page: it is hosted independently of the robot. Robot control is deliberately only possible from the lab network.
